# Value depth audit, 13 October 2026

Kaleb asked for every buyer and seller value to be reviewed one by one, because they are
shallow: each answers its question, then stops. This document is the framework for judging
how much a value is worth to the person using it, the audit of all thirteen values against
it, and the upgrade for each one, ranked by what it buys.

**How this was gathered:** the code at `160e959` (main after PR #12): `lib/core/values.ts`,
every value's page under `app/(rift)/buy`, `app/(rift)/sell` and `app/(rift)/abroad`, and the
engines they call (`compute.ts`, `afford.ts`, `seller.ts`, `assistance.ts`,
`lender-questions.ts`, `abroad.ts`). Every dollar figure in §1 and in each "Today" paragraph
was produced by those engines, not worked out by hand. The "expert gap" paragraphs are what
the page should be able to say; a figure there is an example of the kind of sentence, and `$X`
marks one the upgrade would compute. Blueprint v5 §5 and requirements LEAD and MONEY were read
for what each value was meant to be.

**Not checked:** the live site, real traffic, and any data source named under "Needs". Where
a Georgia rule is quoted, the statute is named so it can be checked before anything is built
on it.

**What shipped with this audit:** `lib/core/value-inputs.test.ts`, which proves that every
question a value asks can change its answer, and records the four that cannot (F1) as a list
that may only shrink.

**Labels** follow `manual-review-2026-10-05.md`. Priority: P0 broken now, P1 a real gain, P2
polish. Size: S under a day, M one to three days, L more. Owner: Eng, Kaleb, Broker.

## 1. Summary

The values are honest and well built. They are shallow for one reason: **each one answers the
literal question and stops at "what"**. None says "so what" (which input matters most, where
the threshold is, what the break-even is) or "now what" (the decision, the deadline, who
decides). And none uses what the person already told another value to say something no single
value could.

Three things make it worse than shallow:

1. **Questions that change nothing.** Four values ask for the county, print it in the answer
   ("on a $325,000 home in DeKalb County"), and use it nowhere: tax is a flat 1% and insurance
   a flat $1,650 in all 159 counties. The person is told the answer was fitted to them. It was
   not. (F1, now enforced by test.)
2. **Defaults stated as facts.** The unclaimed-money page tells every visitor "You have owned
   this home about 11 years" and "Our records question suggests no homestead exemption is on
   this parcel" before they touch anything. There are no records; 11 is a default. (F3, P0.)
3. **The most common loan is computed as a different loan.** The down payment question labels
   3.5% "The FHA minimum", and the engine then charges conventional mortgage insurance on it,
   with no upfront premium and with insurance that comes off. FHA insurance at under 10% down
   stays for the life of the loan. (F2.)

The five upgrades worth most, all computable from answers the person already gives or from one
public dataset:

| # | Upgrade | Value it changes | Why it is worth it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The real ceiling**: the lower of what the payment reaches and what the cash reaches | What fits | A buyer comfortable at $2,400 a month with $20,000 saved is told about $305,000 fits. Their cash reaches $250,000. The page is $55,000 optimistic today |
| 2 | **County property tax and homestead, from the state's millage table** | Cash to close, Monthly cost, What you'd keep, Selling costs, Money you may be losing | Fixes F1 in four values at once, and turns the unclaimed page's hard-coded "$600 to $1,400" into this county's figure |
| 3 | **Break-even price and sensitivity** for sellers | What you'd keep | $400,000 sale, $200,000 owed, 5%: keeps $176,375; every $20,000 of price moves it $18,880; below **$213,162** the sale costs money. One sentence, pure arithmetic, never said today |
| 4 | **"When do you want to buy?" against the computed date** | Timeline | Rule 11 already requires it; the v5 value never asks. Adds the price that moves while they save: 25 months becomes 28 if prices rise 4% a year |
| 5 | **Loan type in the money** (FHA, VA, USDA fees from their published tables) | Cash to close, Monthly cost, What fits | The answer for the commonest first-time buyer is computed on the wrong loan today |

## 2. The framework

### 2.1 What "value" means here: the expert gap

A value is worth exactly the difference between what the person knows after it and what they
knew before it. The ceiling is what a good expert says in ten minutes with the same answers:
Kaleb, a straight-talking lender, a tax preparer. So the measure is:

> **The expert gap:** everything the expert would tell this person, with these answers, that
> the page does not.

A generic calculator closes almost none of it. A page that closes most of it is the
front-loaded value the business is built on: the stranger gets the consult before the
conversation, and the conversation that follows starts from it.

### 2.2 The depth ladder

Each rung includes the ones below it. A value's depth is the highest rung it reaches for a
typical person.

| Rung | Name | The page... | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D0 | Restate | does arithmetic any free calculator does | "Your payment is $2,547" |
| D1 | Itemise | shows where the number comes from, honestly | The five monthly lines, assumptions, failure mode |
| D2 | Localise | uses Georgia and county facts the person could not easily find, sourced and dated | This county's millage; the state's intangible tax on the loan |
| D3 | Personalise | changes materially with every answer, and asks nothing it does not use | Three different buyers get three different stories, not three different totals |
| D4 | Sensitise | shows which input matters most, where the thresholds are, the break-even | "Below $213,162 the sale costs you money"; "mortgage insurance ends around year 12" |
| D5 | Decide | names the decision this enables, the next action, its deadline and who decides | "File homestead by April 1; the tax commissioner decides; here is the form" |
| D6 | Compound | combines answers from several values into something none says alone | Payment ceiling × cash ceiling; sale proceeds as the next home's down payment |

Most values today sit at D1, some reach D2 or D4 in one place. The target for every value is
D4 at least, D5 wherever a decision exists, D6 wherever two values share a person.

### 2.3 The scorecard

Eight dimensions, each scored 0 to 3 against fixed anchors, so two reviewers score the same.
Maximum 24.

| | Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q | The question behind the question | Answers a different question | Answers the literal one | Names the decision it serves | Moves the decision forward |
| I | Inputs earn their place | An asked input changes nothing, or a default is stated as an answer | Some inputs barely matter | Every input matters | Every input matters and nothing material is missing |
| L | Local truth | Generic figures only | Georgia mentioned, not used | One Georgia rule computed | County-level, sourced and dated |
| X | Insight beyond arithmetic | Total only | One comparison | Levers or scenarios | Thresholds, break-evens, the input that matters most |
| A | Next action | None | Generic call to action | A specific next step | Step, deadline and who decides |
| H | Honesty | A figure without basis | Assumptions only | Assumptions and failure mode | Plus unknown is not zero and said is not default |
| C | Compounding | Islanded | Reuses answers to skip questions | Reuses another value's result | Says something only the combination can |
| K | Reason to keep | Nothing changes after today | Something could change | Says what would change it | Worth an alert (the D14 asks-for-details side) |

### 2.4 The method: five steps per value

1. **Write the consult.** For three personas (tight, typical, comfortable; for sellers: thin
   equity, typical, long-held), write what the expert would say in ten minutes. Use real
   figures from the engine for that persona.
2. **Classify every sentence:**
   - **C**, computable now from the person's answers. Build it.
   - **D**, computable with a dataset that has an official source. Source it, date it, give it
     an owner and a re-check window, as the assistance registry does (§6.5, §6.6).
   - **H**, needs a person: Kaleb, a lender, an attorney, the tax commissioner. Turn it into a
     named next step on the page; it is also the honest reason to talk to Kaleb.
   - **G**, would need generation. Dropped. It never reaches the page.
3. **Score** the current page on the scorecard. The gap per dimension is the work list.
4. **Build C, then D, then name H.** Each sentence keeps its assumptions and failure mode.
5. **Prove it** with four tests:
   - *Input efficacy*: every asked input can change the answer
     (`lib/core/value-inputs.test.ts`, shipped).
   - *Persona divergence*: the three personas get materially different findings, not only
     different totals.
   - *Threshold truth*: each threshold or break-even stated on the page, fed back into the
     engine, lands on the boundary it names.
   - *Docs drift*: the rule's statute or source and its figure agree with
     `docs/calculations.md` (`docs.test.ts`).

Then measure the ladder (answer, next value, save) per value with answer-free telemetry. The
framework's bet is that depth raises the save rate; the pilot report is where that is checked.

### 2.5 Where "advanced intelligence" fits

Rule 1 is not a limit on intelligence. It is a limit on generation. The intelligence goes here:

| Where | What it does | Allowed because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Solvers in the value path** | Inverse solves (the price a payment or a savings balance reaches), break-evens, amortisation dates, a search over levers for the smallest change that meets their date | Deterministic, testable, every output traceable to inputs and assumptions |
| **Sourced data behind the solvers** | Millage, exemptions, loan fee tables, fair market rents, program terms | Each record has a source, a check date and a named verifier (rule 9) |
| **AI off the value path** | An agent reads official pages and drafts records (a county's exemption, a program's new cap); a named person verifies before it can be shown | The model proposes; the person stands behind it; nothing unverified is shown (§4.4 of handoff) |
| **AI on the agent's side** | Summarising what a lead worked out, for Kaleb | The customer never sees it as a figure |
| **Not allowed** | A model writing any number, or any sentence containing one, that a customer reads | Rule 1; D30 drops any drafted block containing a digit |

The single most "intelligent" feature this framework proposes is the lever search in §4.4: it
is an optimiser, not a language model, and it is the one a person would pay an adviser for.

## 4. Value by value

Each entry: what it does today, its score, the expert gap for a typical person, and the
upgrade. "Ships" items are computed (C) unless marked (D) for sourced data. Rules named are
the ones the upgrade must keep.

### 4.1 Assistance: "What Georgia programs might help me buy?"

**Today.** Six inputs; matches every recorded program with fits / needs checking per rule,
amount, best potential combination, official source and check date; stale programs withheld
and said so. The strongest value on the site. Gated "My assistance plan" adds steps and
documents.

**Score 17/24** (Q2 I3 L3 X1 A2 H3 C1 K2). Depth D3.

**The expert gap.** "Your income is $6,000 under this program's limit for a household of
three; a raise would not lose it this year, a second earner would." "This one is forgivable
over five years: sell in year three and you repay about $X." "If the house is under $Y, a
second program opens." "With this one approved, your cash to close falls from $24,788 to $Z
and you could buy N months sooner, if the lender says yes."

**Ships.**
- *Distance to every threshold*: income limit for their household, price cap, against their
  answers, in dollars ("$6,000 under", "$15,000 over"). Uses limits already in the records.
- *What it costs to leave early*: for forgivable and deferred programs, the amount repayable
  by year of sale. Needs the forgiveness schedule as a structured field on the record (D; it is
  prose in `terms` today).
- *"If approved" scenario*, below the answer and never above it: cash to close and months to
  ready with the program applied, labelled conditional. MONEY-02 and rule 3 keep it out of
  every headline. **Kaleb decides** whether a conditional figure may appear on the public page
  at all (decision V3).
- *The price that opens another program*: the highest price at which one more match turns from
  no to fits.

**Acceptance.** A household one person larger moves the income distance by the record's own
step; a repayment schedule at year 0 equals the full amount and at the forgiveness year equals
zero; no conditional figure appears in `AnswerHead`. **Size** M (C) + M (D). **Owner** Eng;
V3 Kaleb.

### 4.2 Cash to close: "How much cash do I really need?"

**Today.** Price, down payment, county. Six lines: down payment, closing at a flat 3%,
prepaids, inspection $550, appraisal $650, earnest money (credited back). For $325,000 at 3.5%
in DeKalb: **$24,788**, of which the down payment is $11,375.

**Score 8/24** (Q2 I1 L1 X1 A0 H3 C0 K0). Depth D1. County inert (F1); loan type absent (F2).

**The expert gap.** "Georgia charges an intangible tax on your loan: $941 on yours, and it is
the one line nobody has heard of." "On FHA the upfront premium is added to the loan, so it is
not cash, but it is $5,488 more owed." "Ask the seller for 3% and this falls by $9,750; that
is the most powerful sentence in your offer." "Have $24,788 to close, and about two months of
payments on top so the lender sees reserves."

**Ships.**
- *Named Georgia lines*: intangible recording tax on the loan, $1.50 per $500 (O.C.G.A.
  § 48-6-61), computed exactly, taken out of the 3% rather than added to it.
- *By loan type* (D): FHA upfront premium (financed), VA funding fee, USDA guarantee fee, from
  the agencies' published tables, each with its source and date. Asks `loanType`, which
  already exists, with "Not sure yet" defaulting to the current behaviour and saying so.
- *Seller help*: the total at 0%, 3% and 6% seller contribution, capped at the loan type's
  limit. Pure arithmetic.
- *Before you're approved vs at the table*: the ledger's suggested reserve
  (`lib/core/ledger.ts`, two months) as a second line, "what a lender likes to see left".
  Already computed for the journey; not shown here.
- *County escrow* (D): three months of this county's tax, not a flat 1% (§6).

**Acceptance.** Input efficacy (county comes off `KNOWN_INERT`); the intangible tax at a
$313,625 loan is $941; FHA at 3.5% shows a financed premium and no change to cash; seller help
never takes the total below zero. **Size** M. **Owner** Eng.

### 4.3 Monthly cost: "What would I pay each month?"

**Today.** Price, down payment, county, this week's Freddie Mac rate. Five lines and a band of
three prices ±$40,000. $325,000 at 3.5%: **$2,547** (principal and interest $1,982, tax $271,
insurance $138, mortgage insurance $157).

**Score 8/24** (Q1 I1 L1 X1 A0 H3 C0 K1). Depth D1. County inert; loan type absent.

**The expert gap.** "Only $284 of that first payment is yours; the rest is interest, tax and
insurance." "Mortgage insurance comes off around year 12 if you just pay as scheduled, sooner
if the home gains value; on FHA it never does." "Half a point on the rate is about $100 a
month either way: $2,445 at 6%, $2,652 at 7%." "File homestead and your tax line drops." "Set
aside about $270 a month for repairs; the lender will not tell you that."

**Ships.**
- *What builds equity*: principal in month one and in year one, from the amortisation the
  engine already does.
- *When mortgage insurance ends*: the month the scheduled balance reaches 78% of the price
  (the automatic termination point for conventional loans), and the monthly drop. For FHA,
  the life-of-loan rule at under 10% down. Month 142 on the typical case.
- *Rate sensitivity*: the payment at ±0.5% and ±1%, replacing or beside the price band. Rate
  moves more than price for most buyers between now and the lock.
- *Owning, not only paying*: an optional maintenance set-aside (labelled as a planning rule,
  1% of price a year), and against their current rent if they gave it. `currentRent` is
  already in `BuyerInputs` and never asked.
- *County tax and homestead* (D): this county's millage at 40% assessment, with and without the
  homestead exemption (§6).

**Acceptance.** Input efficacy; the mortgage insurance end month, fed back, has a balance at or
under 78% and the month before does not; rate ±0.5% brackets the headline. **Size** M.
**Owner** Eng.

### 4.4 Timeline: "When could I buy?"

**Today.** Price, down payment, savings, monthly saving. Months to ready, and the best two of
three fixed levers: save $200 more, look $25,000 lower, ask the seller for 2%. Typical: **25
months**. Never says "ready now" for an unknown saving rate.

**Score 12/24** (Q2 I3 L0 X2 A1 H3 C1 K0). Depth D4 in one place (levers).

**The expert gap.** "You said next spring. At this rate it is 25 months. To make spring you
need $1,240 a month, or a $260,000 home, or 3.5% instead of 10% down." "Prices will not wait:
at 4% a year the same home is $352,671 by then, and that makes it 28 months." "If the program
you match approves, it is nine months."

**Ships.**
- *Stated against computed* (rule 11, §4.15): ask "When would you like to buy?" with a
  "Just exploring" option. With a date, say the gap in months and what closes it. With
  "exploring", no comparison. Never quote a date they did not give.
- *The lever search*: for their date, the smallest single change that meets it on each lever
  (monthly saving, price, down payment, seller help), solved exactly, not three fixed steps.
  This is the optimiser of §2.5.
- *The moving target*: months recomputed with the price rising at a labelled planning rate,
  the same 4% the abroad engine already uses, shown as a second line, never the headline.
- *Conditional assistance* as a lever, labelled, under V3.

**Acceptance.** Persona with no stated date shows no "you said"; each solved lever, applied,
meets the date and one step less does not; the moving-target line is never shorter than the
fixed one. **Size** M. **Owner** Eng.

### 4.5 What fits: "How much home fits me?"

**Today.** Comfortable payment, income, debts, down payment. The price the payment reaches,
beside the 28% and 36% guidelines; says when the payment is above both. Never states a
lender's maximum (MONEY-05). Income and debts only feed the guidelines, not the headline.

**Score 10/24** (Q2 I2 L0 X2 A1 H3 C0 K0). Depth D2.

**The expert gap.** "Your payment reaches about $305,000. Your $20,000 reaches $250,000 once
closing costs are in. Today, cash is your ceiling, not the payment." "Every $5,000 more saved
lifts it about $X." "At 6% instead of 6.5% your payment reaches $Y."

**Ships.**
- *The real ceiling* (D6): the lower of the payment-limited price and the cash-limited price
  (the inverse of cash to close at their savings), and which one binds. Asks `savings`, already
  shared with Timeline, so most people answer nothing new.
- *What lifts it*: dollars of price per $1,000 saved (when cash binds) or per $100 of payment
  (when the payment binds).
- *Rate sensitivity* as in 4.3.

**Acceptance.** When savings are ample the ceiling equals today's figure; the cash-limited
price has a cash to close at or under savings and $5,000 more does not; the page never says
"afford" or "approved". **Size** S to M. **Owner** Eng.

### 4.6 Lender questions: "What should I ask a lender?"

**Today.** Price, down payment, first-time status. Ten or so questions in four groups, each
with why; adds program questions when assistance answers exist. Printable.

**Score 10/24** (Q2 I2 L0 X0 A2 H2 C2 K0). Depth D1. It prepares the meeting; it does not help
judge what comes out of it.

**The expert gap.** "Bring me both Loan Estimates. This one is cheaper for five years, that one
after; how long will you keep the loan?" "Two points to save 0.375% pays back in month 71."
"Section B is shoppable, Section A is not."

**Ships.**
- *Loan Estimate comparison*: the person types five lines from each of two Loan Estimates
  (rate, points, origination charges, lender credits, cash to close). Computed: total cost at
  3, 5 and 7 years, the crossover month, and the points break-even month. Pure arithmetic on
  numbers they hold. This turns the value from a list into a decision.
- *Loan-type questions*: FHA (life-of-loan insurance under 10% down), VA (funding fee
  exemption with a disability rating), USDA (area eligibility), when `loanType` is known.

**Acceptance.** Identical estimates have no crossover; the break-even month, fed back, is the
first month cumulative savings exceed the points. **Size** M. **Owner** Eng.

### 4.7 What you'd keep: "What would I actually keep?"

**Today.** County, price, amount owed, commission. Price minus payoff and costs, commission as
their number or left out and priced per 1%; moving and unagreed concessions excluded; a
shortfall said as one (MONEY-06, §4.16). $400,000, $200,000 owed, 5%: **$176,375**.

**Score 9/24** (Q2 I1 L1 X1 A1 H3 C0 K0). Depth D1. County inert.

**The expert gap.** "Below $213,162 this sale costs you money; that is your floor in any
negotiation." "Every $20,000 of price is $18,880 to you; every point of commission is $4,000."
"You have owned it 15 years: check the gain against the $250,000 exclusion before you pick a
date." "Each month it sits costs you about $X in payment, tax and insurance." "This is the
down payment on your next home: here is what it buys."

**Ships.**
- *Break-even price*: the price at which the net is zero, solved exactly. MONEY-06 bans an
  invented valuation; this is their own costs, not a value for the house.
- *Sensitivity on their own number*: net at their price ±5% and at commission ±1 point,
  labelled "if it sells for", never "it will sell for".
- *Carrying cost per month listed*: asks their monthly mortgage payment (optional); payment
  plus tax plus insurance per month on the market.
- *The gain against the exclusion*: asks what they paid and when (optional). Gain = price −
  costs − what they paid; compared with $250,000 / $500,000 (26 U.S.C. § 121); said as "worth
  checking with a tax preparer", never as tax owed. `yearsOwned` exists and no seller value
  asks it.
- *The next home* (D6): the net as a down payment, linked into the buyer values with the price
  it reaches. Today the plan is deliberately one side per device; a seller who is also buying
  has two plans. **Kaleb decides** whether a sell-then-buy plan exists (V5).
- *County tax share* (D): the prorated tax at this county's millage.

**Acceptance.** Net at the break-even price is between −$1 and +$1; sensitivity is symmetric
around their price; nothing on the page reads as a valuation. **Size** M. **Owner** Eng; V5
Kaleb.

### 4.8 Money you may be losing: "Am I losing money on my home already?"

**Today.** A client-side page with county, worth, years owned and two checkboxes. Lists
homestead, age-based exemption, assessment check, capital gains exclusion and prepayment
penalty, each with a fixed range and who decides.

**Score 8/24** (Q3 I0 L1 X1 A2 H0 C0 K1). Depth D1. The question is the best on the site; the
answer is the least computed. F3, F4, F5, F6.

**The expert gap.** "In DeKalb, filing homestead saves you about $X a year on a home like
yours, and the deadline is April 1." "Your notice arrives in spring; you have 45 days from its
date to appeal." "At 65 this county exempts school tax entirely: that is about $Y a year."
"Georgia now caps how fast a homesteaded home's assessment can rise, unless your county opted
out; yours did not."

**Ships.**
- *P0, now*: no sentence quotes a default. "You have owned this home about 11 years" appears
  only after they set it; "Our records question suggests" becomes "You told us no homestead is
  filed", shown only after they answer. (F3)

### 4.9 Selling costs: "What will selling cost me?"
