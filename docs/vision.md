# Rift Product Vision

**Status:** Interview-validated product direction  
**Audience:** Everyone designing, building, operating, or representing Rift  
**Graded by:** [benchmark.md](benchmark.md)

## The future Rift is building

Residential real estate should not feel like a collection of disconnected forms, inboxes, vendors, deadlines, and unexplained decisions.

Rift exists to make the complete buyer and seller lifecycle clear, coordinated, and personal. Customers should always understand where they are, what happens next, who is responsible, and when professional judgment is required. Agents should be able to deliver that experience to more clients without losing attention, warmth, or control.

Rift's long-term ambition is to become the most trusted client experience and agent operating platform for residential real estate.

## Mission

Make every step of buying or selling a home easier for the customer while helping the agent serve more people with exceptional consistency.

## The growth loop

Rift is not only a service platform. It is a growth system, and the service quality is what makes the growth compound. The loop is the product:

```text
Generate a lead
  -> Run the operation smoothly for the client and the agent
    -> Win and close the sale
      -> Earn advocacy
        -> Generate the next lead
```

Three commitments hold the loop together:

1. **Overload the front end.** Rift's lead generation strategy is not persuasion or tracking. It is giving away more real value, earlier, than anyone else will. A stranger should finish seven minutes with Rift holding something they would have paid for. Conversion is the byproduct of that, not of a technique. The assessment package and Rift Offer both work fully without an account.
2. **Operational excellence as marketing.** A client who always knows their position tells other people about it. Clarity is the referral mechanism, not a feature next to it.
3. **The closing is a beginning.** Every closing must produce a review, a referral path, and a durable reason to return. A closing that produces none of those is a failed closing, regardless of the commission.

Design decisions are ranked by their effect on this loop. A surface that does not generate, retain, convert, or compound a relationship is a candidate for deletion.

## Who Rift serves

### Buyers

Rift supports buyers from early readiness through financing preparation, home search, offers, contract, closing, move-in, and the first year of ownership.

### Sellers

Rift supports sellers from the first thought of selling through property preparation, pricing, listing, offer review, contract, closing, moving, and post-sale follow-up.

### Agents

Rift gives the agent one place to understand every relationship, prioritize attention, approve important work, coordinate transactions, and automate repeatable service.

### Households and collaborators

One transaction may involve spouses, co-buyers, co-sellers, lenders, attorneys, transaction coordinators, inspectors, photographers, contractors, appraisers, insurance agents, artists, and gift vendors. Rift coordinates their responsibilities without exposing information they do not need.

## The customer promise

Every customer should be able to answer five questions immediately:

1. Where am I in the process?
2. What is the most important thing I need to do next?
3. What is the agent or another professional doing for me?
4. What decision, document, appointment, or deadline is approaching?
5. Where can I ask for help?

The customer experience is a personal journey command center, not a conventional dashboard. It organizes lifecycle progress, the next action, responsibilities, deadlines, appointments, documents, decisions, and help around one shared picture.

## The agent promise

The agent should begin each day knowing:

- Who needs attention now.
- What is late, blocked, risky, or waiting for approval.
- Which customers reached an important milestone.
- What Rift completed automatically.
- Which conversations and appointments are next.
- Where the agent's professional judgment will create the most value.

The default agent experience is an attention-based command center. Buyer and seller pipelines remain available, but they do not dictate the agent's day.

## The Rift product model

Rift owns the journey and coordinates the tools around it.

Rift is the source of truth for the customer relationship, lifecycle, tasks, responsibilities, milestones, documents, decisions, deadlines, permissions, and activity history. Email, text messaging, calendars, electronic signatures, MLS data, lending, accounting, closing services, and vendors connect through integrations where practical.

Rift is not an MLS, lender, law firm, credit bureau, electronic-signature provider, or accounting system. It makes those services easier to coordinate and understand.

## What Rift replaces

An agent today pays for several tools that each own one fragment of the relationship, and stitches them together by memory. Rift's claim is not that it adds another surface; it is that it takes ownership of the journey the others fragment.

| Category the agent pays for today | What Rift takes over | What may remain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead capture forms and landing pages | Entry surfaces, assessment, capture, attribution | Paid ad platforms |
| Generic calculators and valuation sites | Personal, Georgia-specific, assumption-transparent answers | Public data sources |
| CRM and drip marketing | Relationships, lifecycle stage, attention queue, nurture | Bulk email sending service |
| Transaction coordination software | Dates, tasks, documents, owners, collaborator access | Human transaction coordinator, now working inside Rift |
| Client-facing status spreadsheets and update emails | Journey command center | Nothing |
| Offer intake by email attachment | Rift Offer with structured preparation | E-signature provider |
| Post-closing mailers and pop-by tracking | Property Passport, referral and review engine | Gift and print vendors |

The honest boundary: Rift does not replace the MLS, the lender, the closing attorney, the e-signature provider, or accounting. It replaces the agent's habit of holding all of it together in their head.

## Agentic-native foundation

Agentic behavior is infrastructure, not a chatbot added later.

Rift should be able to understand new information, prepare a plan, identify the next best action, execute permitted routine work, monitor the result, recover from failure, and improve reusable workflows from agent-approved corrections.

Customers experience one coherent Rift service. They do not need to see a collection of bots or technical AI terminology. Customer-facing language uses clear states such as:

- Preliminary estimate
- Pending review
- Reviewed by your agent
- Verified by lender
- Based on information provided
- Updated on a specific date
- Subject to professional review

Behind that coherent experience, specialized capabilities may handle intake, planning, documents, meetings, offers, transactions, communications, prioritization, and post-closing care.

## Human judgment and configurable autonomy

Rift supports three operating modes:

1. **Review everything:** Rift prepares work and the agent approves it.
2. **Balanced:** Rift completes routine work and requests approval for important decisions.
3. **High automation:** Rift runs trusted workflows independently within explicit permissions and limits.

The agent can adjust authority by workflow, client, communication channel, connected service, and spending limit. Pricing, lending readiness, offer interpretation, contract actions, major lifecycle changes, fair-housing-sensitive decisions, and other professional judgments retain safeguards regardless of mode.

Some actions never become automatic in any mode: presenting or declining to present an offer, signing or sending an agreement, committing the agent to a price opinion, and any communication that states a legal or lending conclusion.

## One lifecycle, personalized execution

Buyer and seller stages remain canonical so customers, agents, reporting, and connected tools share the same language. Agents personalize the experience by adding milestones, tasks, resources, Decision Rooms, and Lifecycle Playbooks inside those stages.

This distinction matters: Rift should be flexible without making every agent invent a different definition of buying or selling.

## Signature capabilities

### Value-first front end

Before anyone has an account, Rift hands over a complete, personal, useful package. For a buyer that is a Georgia assistance match, true cash to close, the cash gap and the months to close it, all-in monthly cost across a price band, the single blocker to preapproval, a personalized lender question sheet, and a dated plan. For a seller it is estimated net proceeds with the full Georgia cost stack, repair return triage, timing, sequencing, a disclosure checklist, and a dated plan.

The strongest asset in that package is the **Georgia assistance match**. Most first-time buyers do not know that Georgia Dream, county and city programs, and FHLB Atlanta exist, or that they might qualify for meaningful money. Telling them, for free, in the first seven minutes, is worth more than any advertisement Rift could buy — and it is a genuinely good thing to do whether or not they ever become a client.

The seller equivalent is **estimated net proceeds** — every valuation site answers what a home is worth, and almost none answer what the owner walks away with — paired with an **unclaimed value check** for exemptions never filed, assessments worth appealing, and payoff surprises worth finding early.

Every number is preliminary, shows its assumptions, is editable, and says how it could be wrong. Rift never implies qualification or approval, and every figure is **computed from a calculation engine and a verified registry, never generated**. That rule is what makes giving this much away affordable, accurate, and safe to leave open to the public at the same time.

The package is also built to travel. It is a shareable artifact, not a screen: a co-buyer who never took the assessment, a relative being asked to gift a down payment, and a lender who needs the situation all receive a version scoped to them. Every forward is distribution Rift did not pay for, and the person a buyer shares it with is usually the person they cannot move forward without.

### Decision Room

A shared place to understand and record an important decision. Buyers can compare affordability, properties, offer strategies, inspection outcomes, and closing scenarios. Sellers can compare offers by estimated net proceeds, terms, timing, contingencies, and risk. The agent adds context and controls release where configured.

### Rift Offer

An account-free offer submission experience available for any property address. Rift-managed listings receive a confirmed, listing-specific presentation. A submitter uploads the offer PDF first; Rift prepares the important terms, the submitter reviews them, optionally indicates whether proof of funds or preapproval is included, and submits the original documents with a structured summary.

Every submitted offer reaches the agent first. The agent decides whether to present it, hold it, or decline to present it, and that decision is recorded. Rift never releases an offer to a seller before the agent has seen it, and never discards one.

Rift Offer is also a lead source. Every submission introduces a cooperating agent or an unrepresented buyer, and each becomes a tracked relationship.

### Meeting-to-Plan

Meeting notes or consented recordings become a proposed recap and set of plan changes. Rift identifies facts, decisions, commitments, unanswered questions, tasks, and follow-ups; the agent reviews the differences before publishing an updated client plan.

### Document-to-Journey

Contracts, disclosures, inspections, lender documents, and other files become proposed dates, responsibilities, tasks, risks, and missing-information requests. Original files remain unchanged, and consequential changes require review.

### Lifecycle Playbooks

Agents create reusable workflows triggered by lifecycle events. A playbook may create a task, prepare communication, request a document, schedule follow-up, coordinate a vendor, or purchase a gift within approved limits. Each playbook can run manually, with approval, or automatically.

### Rift Property Passport

After closing, the transaction becomes a durable property record containing documents, warranties, improvements, maintenance, vendors, receipts, property history, and future home-care guidance. It gives customers a reason to keep Rift and gives the agent a useful long-term relationship layer.

## Trust principles

1. Be useful before being impressive.
2. Show the next meaningful action before secondary metrics.
3. Never silently lose an offer, document, deadline, message, purchase, or workflow action.
4. Label estimates, assumptions, review state, source, and verification clearly.
5. Collect only information needed for the journey and explain sensitive requests.
6. Give every task a visible owner: customer, agent, Rift, or outside professional.
7. Preserve original documents and a complete activity history.
8. Protect private information through least-privilege access.
9. Keep protected characteristics and their proxies out of ranking and prioritization.
10. Build customer capability and trust, not dependency or manufactured urgency.

## Fair housing in practice

Principle 9 is only real if it has a mechanism. Rift enforces it three ways.

**Prohibited inputs.** The following never enter any ranking, prioritization, scoring, routing, or automated recommendation: race, color, national origin, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, familial status, disability, age, marital status, source of income, and any recognized proxy for them, including name-derived inference, photographs, spoken or written language preference, school ratings, neighborhood demographic composition, and crime statistics.

**Permitted inputs.** Prioritization may use only lifecycle stage, deadline proximity, task and document status, committed dates, response latency, transaction value at risk, and explicit client-stated preferences about property attributes.

**Audit.** The set of inputs feeding any prioritization or recommendation is enumerable and reviewed quarterly. Any change to that set requires an explicit review. Rift never suggests, filters, or ranks *neighborhoods* for a client; it presents properties the client or agent selected.

Rift also never generates copy describing a community's people, and steers clear of "good area," "family neighborhood," and similar language in any prepared communication.

## Compliance posture

Rift operates inside a licensed profession and is built to leave a defensible record.

- Rift is a tool used by a licensed agent under brokerage supervision; it does not perform licensed activity on its own.
- Every offer received, every presentation decision, and every reason is timestamped and preserved, so the agent can demonstrate what was received and what was done with it.
- Transaction records follow brokerage retention requirements; client-facing personal data can be exported and deleted on request within those limits.
- Meeting capture requires clear participant consent before recording.
- Rift prepares communications; it does not send anything that states a legal, lending, appraisal, or valuation conclusion without the agent.

## Initial market and expansion

Rift begins in Georgia with realistic local workflows, terminology, contracts, closing practices, assistance programs, and service-provider relationships. The architecture should allow geographic expansion without pretending that one state's process applies everywhere.

Property data is a gate, not a footnote. MLS and IDX access is license-bound and rule-bound; Rift is designed to work usefully with agent-entered and client-entered property information first, and to treat MLS integration as an enhancement that arrives when access is actually granted. No core flow may become unusable without it.

## Who Rift is for, and when

Rift is built **single-tenant first, multi-tenant by architecture**.

**Now:** one agent, one Georgia market, one brand relationship: *Rift, guided by Kaleb*. Every decision optimizes for that agent closing more transactions with less strain. This is the real-world proving ground, and its friction is the most valuable input the product has.

**Next:** additional agents on the same product, each with their own clients, branding, playbooks, and autonomy settings. Nothing in the data model, permissions, or lifecycle may assume a single agent, even while only one exists.

**Later:** brokerage tenancy with shared standards, approved playbooks, team assignment, and performance reporting, with co-branding alongside Rift as the trusted product identity.

The trap to avoid is building a personal tool that cannot become a product, or a generic product that serves nobody well enough to prove the loop. Single-tenant behavior, multi-tenant bones.

## Business model

Agents or brokerages pay for Rift. Buyers and sellers receive the core journey experience without charge.

Working assumptions, to be revised with real data:

- Per-agent subscription in the range of a professional tool an agent already buys, positioned against the combined cost of the CRM, transaction manager, and client-update effort it replaces.
- The free client experience is a customer-acquisition cost carried by the agent's subscription, not a separate product.
- Variable cost is real and must be watched. Document preparation, meeting processing, and agentic execution carry per-use cost, and clients are free. Every such capability needs a per-relationship and per-month cost ceiling, visible usage, and graceful degradation to manual entry when a ceiling is reached.
- Referral or partner compensation, if introduced, must be disclosed honestly and must not determine customer guidance.

## What success looks like

For customers, success means understanding their current position and next action without needing an explanation.

For the agent, success means finding and completing the day's highest-value work within minutes, serving more active clients, reducing missed details, and delivering a consistent experience that still feels personal.

For Rift, success means that every assessment, meeting, document, offer, decision, milestone, and post-closing interaction strengthens one shared and trustworthy journey.

Success is measured, not asserted. The scoring instrument and the field metrics that grade real-world use are in [benchmark.md](benchmark.md). The three that matter most:

1. **Advocacy share of pipeline.** New leads arriving from referral or repeat business, targeting 30 percent by month 12. This is the loop closing.
2. **Consultation booking rate.** Snapshots that become a real conversation with the agent, targeting 25 percent. This is value-first capture actually working.
3. **Active clients served well.** Concurrent active clients carried without the stale-task rate or the status-question rate rising. This is leverage, and it is the only honest measure of it.
