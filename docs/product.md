## Front-end value

Rift's lead generation strategy is not better tracking. It is **giving away more real value, earlier, than anyone else will.** A stranger should finish seven minutes with Rift holding something they would have paid for, and should feel slightly uncomfortable that it was free. Conversion is the byproduct of that feeling, not of a persuasion technique.

Attribution and campaign connection matter, but they are plumbing. They need to be solid, not exceptional. The front-end value is where Rift competes.

### The value ladder

Value escalates with commitment, and every rung gives more than it takes.

| Rung | What the visitor gives | What Rift gives back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Instant tools | Nothing | A single real answer in under a minute |
| 1. Assessment | Five to seven minutes, no account | The complete readiness or reality package |
| 2. Saved snapshot | Email and a password | A shareable document, a living plan, and alerts when the numbers move |
| 3. Consultation | A conversation | Agent-reviewed numbers, verified eligibility, and a published plan |

Two rules hold the ladder up. Nothing at a lower rung is withheld to force the next one — the full package appears before the save is ever mentioned. And each rung is genuinely useful if the visitor stops there.

### Instant tools

Each tool answers one question, works alone in under a minute, and is its own campaign destination. Each also opens directly into the matching assessment for someone who wants the full picture.

| Tool | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Georgia assistance check | Is there money available to me that I don't know about? |
| True cash to close | How much do I actually need, not just the down payment? |
| All-in monthly payment | What will this really cost me every month? |
| Rent versus buy crossover | At what point does buying beat what I pay now? |
| Net proceeds estimator | If I sell, what do I actually walk away with? |
| Repair return triage | Is fixing this worth it before I list? |
| Buy-before-sell sequencing | Which do I do first, and what does each order cost me? |

### The buyer readiness package

Produced by the buyer assessment, before any account:

1. **Georgia assistance match.** Named programs the buyer may qualify for, with estimated amounts, program type, and what each requires. Georgia Dream, county and city programs, FHLB Atlanta, and employer or gift sources. This is the strongest single asset Rift has: most first-time buyers do not know this money exists, and finding it is worth real dollars to them immediately.
2. **True cash to close.** Down payment, closing costs, prepaids, earnest money, inspection, appraisal, and moving — not the down payment alone, which is the number that misleads almost every first-time buyer.
3. **The cash gap and the time to close it.** What is still uncovered, and at the buyer's stated savings rate, how many months that takes — with the two specific changes that shorten it most.
4. **All-in monthly across a price band.** Three price points with full principal, interest, taxes, insurance, PMI, and HOA, so the buyer sees the shape of the trade-off rather than one number.
5. **The one blocker.** The single most consequential thing standing between this buyer and a lender preapproval, named plainly.
6. **The lender question sheet.** The exact questions this buyer should ask a lender, personalized to their situation, ready to take into any conversation. It costs Rift nothing and it is often the most appreciated item in the package.
7. **A dated ninety-day action plan** with an owner on every step.

### The seller reality package

Produced by the seller assessment, before any account:

1. **Estimated net proceeds.** The full Georgia cost stack: mortgage payoff, commission, transfer tax, recording and intangibles, prorated taxes, likely concessions, repairs, and moving. Every valuation site answers *what is my home worth*; almost none answer *what do I walk away with*, which is the question the seller actually has.
2. **Repair return triage.** What pays back in their price band, what does not, and what only matters for photographs.
3. **Timing analysis.** Listing now versus later, measured against their stated goal rather than against a generic seasonal chart.
4. **Sequencing.** For a seller who is also buying, the order of operations and what each path costs in money, risk, and disruption.
5. **Unclaimed value check.** The seller-side equivalent of the buyer's assistance match: money the owner may already be leaving on the table. Homestead and age-based exemptions they may not have filed, an assessment that may be worth appealing and the window to do it, prepayment penalties or payoff surprises worth confirming early, transferable warranties that add value at sale, and the primary-residence capital gains exclusion and whether their occupancy history supports it.
6. **Disclosure preparation checklist.** Georgia-specific, so nothing is discovered late.
7. **A dated thirty-day preparation plan** with an owner on every step.

The unclaimed value check is framed strictly as *worth confirming*, never as advice. Rift is not a tax advisor, an attorney, or an appraiser. Every item names who actually decides — the county tax office, a tax professional, the lender, the closing attorney — and Rift's role is limited to noticing that the question is worth asking. Exemption and appeal information is held in the same verified registry as the assistance programs, under the rules below, because it goes stale the same way.

### The package as a distribution object

The package is not a screen. It is an artifact designed to be forwarded, and every forward is distribution Rift did not pay for.

Every completed package becomes a shareable link and a branded document, sendable without the recipient creating an account. Sharing is offered as a natural next step rather than a growth prompt, because the people a buyer or seller needs to share with are the people they cannot move forward without.

| Recipient | Why they receive it | What they see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Co-buyer or co-seller | They did not take the assessment and do not yet believe the numbers | The full package, with an invitation to join the journey as a household member |
| A parent or relative gifting funds | They are being asked for money without knowing the real figure | The cash-to-close breakdown and the exact gap, without the household's income or credit details |
| A lender | They need the buyer's situation, and the buyer needs to sound prepared | The situation summary and the lender question sheet, carrying the agent's identity |
| A friend or colleague | They are in the same position and it is the most natural referral there is | The package as an example, with a path to run their own |

Rules:

- A shared package is read-only and carries no personal contact information the sharer did not choose to include.
- The gifter view is scoped deliberately: the cash requirement and the gap, never income, credit, or debt detail. This is a common family conversation and Rift should make it possible without exposing what the buyer would not say out loud.
- A recipient who runs their own assessment is credited to the sharer, exactly like a referral.
- The lender question sheet carries the agent's identity because a loan officer receiving a prepared, well-organized buyer forms an opinion about who is behind it. This is agent-to-professional marketing that costs nothing.
- Every share is visible to the agent as an activity, because a buyer sharing a package with a lender is a strong buying signal.

### The co-buyer

Most buyers are two people, and the second one usually did not take the assessment. Household is not only a permissions concept; the person who did not answer the questions is the person most likely to stall the decision.

- Every buyer and seller journey asks early whether anyone else is part of the decision.
- A named co-buyer receives the package and a genuine invitation to join, not a notification.
- A co-buyer who joins gets their own view of the same journey, can complete the parts of the assessment only they can answer — their savings, their employment, their credit picture — and the package updates to reflect both people.
- Disagreement is a supported state. Where the two answer differently on timing, budget, or priorities, Rift shows both positions plainly rather than averaging them, and surfaces it to the agent as something to resolve in conversation.
- Household members see the journey by default and the sensitive financial detail only where each person has agreed to share it.

### The living package

A snapshot is a photograph of a moving situation, and it starts going stale the moment it is delivered. Rift re-delivers the value instead of drip-marketing around it.

A saved package updates and notifies when:

- A **new assistance program opens** in the customer's county, or one they matched reopens funding. This is the strongest re-engagement message Rift has and it is unambiguously a service.
- A **matched program's terms change** in a way that affects their numbers, including one closing.
- **Rates move** enough to change the monthly payment or the cash gap materially.
- The customer's **own progress** changes the picture: savings reached a threshold, a readiness step completed, a gap closed.
- A **stated assumption goes stale**, such as a target price set six months ago.
- For sellers, an **assessment notice or appeal window** approaches, or a payoff assumption ages past usefulness.

Rules:

- Every update leads with what changed and what it means for them, not with a marketing message.
- Nothing is sent that has no change to report. Silence is correct when nothing moved.
- Frequency is capped so that even a volatile market cannot turn service into noise.
- Each update offers a single, specific next action.
- Updates continue through nurture and into post-closing, where they become the Property Passport's reason to exist.

This is what makes nurture defensible. Rift is not checking in; it is telling someone their situation changed.

### Rules for front-end value

Overloading the front end means putting many numbers in front of a stranger who has verified nothing. That is the risk, and these rules are what keep the value from becoming a liability.

1. **Progressive revelation.** Results appear and update as the visitor answers, not only at the end. Someone who abandons at question nine still leaves with something. This is the largest single lever on completion.
2. **Every assumption is visible and editable.** The value is in the transparency, not in false precision. A buyer who can change the rate assumption and watch the number move trusts it more than one who cannot.
3. **Every number is labeled preliminary,** with its date, its source, and the verification path that would make it real.
4. **Every number states how it could be wrong.** Assistance programs change, rates move, and property taxes vary by parcel. Saying so is part of the value.
5. **Nothing is gated.** No email wall between a visitor and a result they were promised.
6. **Nothing overstates.** Assistance amounts are ranges with eligibility conditions, never approvals. Rift never implies qualification, and the language distinguishes *may qualify for* from *qualifies for*.
7. **Fair housing bounds everything.** All guidance is based on price, financing, property attributes, cost, and timing. Rift never characterizes an area, its people, or its schools, and never ranks or filters neighborhoods.
8. **Front-end value is computed, not generated.** Every number a stranger sees comes from the calculation engine and the verified data registry. Language may be assembled from templates around those numbers. Generative capability is reserved for agent-facing work behind a rate limit — document extraction and meeting processing — and never sits between a visitor and a figure they will make a decision on. This single rule is what makes overloading the front end affordable, accurate, and safe to leave open to the public at once: computed answers have no marginal cost, cannot hallucinate a program that does not exist, and cannot be driven up by abuse.

### Enforcing delivery integrity

Rules four through six above are only real if a violation can be detected. Integrity is checked, not trusted:

- Every customer-facing figure is rendered through a single value component that requires an assumption reference, a review state, and an as-of date. A figure without them cannot render.
- A build-time check fails when a numeric literal is displayed outside that component on any customer-facing surface.
- Every displayed program carries a registry identifier and its verification date; a figure with neither is a build failure, not a styling issue.
- Language templates are reviewed against a prohibited-phrasing list covering approval, qualification, guarantee, valuation, and area-characterization language.
- The set of assumptions behind any package is enumerable and exportable, so what a customer was shown on a given date can be reconstructed exactly.

The absence of this check is why delivery integrity cannot be graded above a 3 on assertion alone.

### Keeping the program data honest

The assistance match is the strongest asset in the front end and the easiest one to turn into damage. Program amounts, income limits, funding availability, and eligibility rules change without notice, and this number is the *first* thing a stranger learns about Rift. Getting it wrong is worse than not offering it.

The same is true of the seller-side exemption, appeal-window, and cost data. Both live in one **program registry**, a maintained data asset with rules, not a seed file:

- Every program carries a **last-verified date**, the source consulted, and the person who verified it.
- Every program shown to a customer displays *verified as of* that date.
- A program not re-verified within the configured window is automatically suppressed from
  customer-facing matching and raised to the agent as a task. The window is a business rule
  (`registryDays`, default **90 days**), not a constant — read it, do not hard-code it.
  Suppression is silent to the customer; a stale program simply does not appear.
- Amounts are shown as ranges with their eligibility conditions attached, never as a single figure and never as an approval.
- Income limits, purchase-price caps, first-time-buyer definitions, occupancy requirements, and repayment or forgiveness terms are part of the record, not footnotes.
- Every match states plainly that eligibility is determined by the program administrator and the lender, not by Rift.
- Funding exhaustion is a first-class state. A program with closed funding shows as closed with its expected reopening, rather than disappearing or appearing available.

When a program's terms change, every customer whose package included it is flagged for a corrected update. Rift never leaves a stale number standing in a plan someone is making decisions against.

### Speed to lead

Front-end value only converts if the human arrives while the visitor still cares.

- The package is delivered instantly and automatically. There is no waiting for the agent to produce value.
- Booking is offered inside the snapshot, at the moment of highest interest, not on a later page.
- A saved snapshot alerts the agent in real time.
- Target for a personal agent touch: within fifteen minutes during working hours, and by the start of the next business day otherwise.
- Every unbooked snapshot enters nurture the same day.

### Proof

Front-end value is only credible if its source is. Public surfaces carry the agent's identity and real evidence: recent closings, genuine client reviews, and short client stories with permission. The referral and reputation engine is what produces this material, so proof and advocacy are the same loop viewed from two ends.

Rift never fabricates, aggregates, or embellishes proof, and never displays a review it did not receive honestly.

## Entry and account creation

The main public page offers **I'm buying a home** and **I'm selling a home**. Each dedicated page also works independently for campaigns and referrals.

Customers can begin a five-to-seven-minute assessment without an account. When saving the preliminary snapshot, Rift reuses the information already collected and asks only for a password. The customer can correct the prefilled information before finishing.

The assessment presents one focused topic at a time, explains why sensitive information is useful, shows progress, supports save-and-return, and provides an **I don't know** option where appropriate.

### Abandonment and resume

Most people who start an assessment will not finish it in one sitting, and that population is the largest single source of lost leads. Rift treats abandonment as a normal state, not a failure.

- Assessment progress persists locally from the first answered question, without an account.
- Returning to the same device resumes at the last unanswered question with prior answers intact.
- Once an email address has been provided and the visitor has explicitly agreed to be contacted about their assessment, an abandoned assessment becomes a resumable link Rift can send once, plus one follow-up if unopened. Rift never emails someone who did not provide an address for this purpose.
- Abandoned assessments are visible to the agent as partial relationships showing progress reached, last topic, source, and any contact information given, so the agent can decide whether a personal touch is worthwhile.
- An abandoned assessment with no contact information is discarded after **30 days** — long
  enough to resume on the same device, short enough that Rift is not holding a collection of
  strangers' finances. The full retention schedule is `RETENTION` in `lib/core/privacy.ts`
  and is shown to the customer rather than buried in a policy page.

## Lead sources and attribution

Every relationship in Rift carries its origin. Without this, no channel can be evaluated and no referral can be credited.

Each relationship records:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| First-touch source | The channel that produced the first visit: referral, repeat client, paid, organic, social, workshop, open house, sign, direct, partner, Rift Offer |
| Campaign and medium | The specific campaign or piece of outreach, captured from link parameters where present |
| Capturing surface | Which Rift surface converted the visit: buyer page, seller page, main page, Rift Offer, direct agent entry |
| Referrer | The person or relationship who sent them, when applicable, as a link to that record |
| First touch timestamp | When the relationship began |
| Agent-corrected source | The agent can correct or set source at any time; the original captured value is preserved alongside it |

Rules:

- Attribution is first-touch and immutable once set, with agent corrections recorded as corrections rather than overwrites.
- Where a link parameter is absent, Rift asks the client once, conversationally, during or after the assessment: *how did you hear about us?* An unanswered question leaves the source as unknown rather than guessing.
- Referral links are per-relationship, so a past client's link credits them automatically.
- Source is a first-class filter on the pipeline, the client directory, and reporting.

## Nurture for leads who are not ready

A large share of captured leads are six to eighteen months from a transaction. They are not a problem to be filtered out; they are the pipeline, and the Exploring and Building readiness stages exist to hold them.

- Every not-yet-ready lead has a stated target timeframe and a readiness plan with real steps.
- A nurture cadence runs against that plan rather than against the calendar: a touch when a readiness step is due, when a plan assumption goes stale, when a relevant local condition changes, and a light periodic check otherwise.
- Default cadence is monthly for leads inside twelve months, quarterly beyond that, and immediate when the client acts on their own plan.
- Every touch carries something useful: a completed step, an updated estimate, a specific next action, or a genuinely relevant local update. Rift does not send contentless check-ins.
- A lead can pause or reduce contact without leaving the journey, and that state is visible to the agent.
- A lead who takes no action across three consecutive touches is surfaced to the agent as stalled, with a suggested personal contact.

## Assessment outputs

Every completed assessment produces:

1. An immediate preliminary journey snapshot for the customer.
2. A concise agent case summary with important facts, risks, and missing information.
3. A proposed plan for agent review.
4. An agent-reviewed plan published after review or consultation.

The customer-facing snapshot is the buyer readiness package or the seller reality package defined above, delivered in full and without an account.

## Consultation booking

The snapshot is useful on its own, which is what makes the next step credible. But a snapshot does not become a client; a conversation does. Consultation booking is a designed moment, not a button in a footer.

- The snapshot ends with a specific, earned invitation tied to what the assessment actually found: the client's single largest obstacle and what the agent would do about it.
- Booking shows real availability and requires only a time and a phone number or preferred channel. No forms beyond what the assessment already collected.
- The client can choose a short introductory call or a full planning session, and can decline while keeping the snapshot and the plan.
- A declined or unbooked snapshot enters nurture rather than disappearing.
- Booked consultations appear in the agent's daily brief with the case summary attached, so the agent walks in prepared.
- After the consultation, Meeting-to-Plan converts the conversation into the published plan, closing the loop between the invitation and the value promised.
- Booking rate is tracked by source, because it is the clearest signal of lead quality per channel.

## Representation and agreements

Representation is the gate between a lead and a client, and it is now the most consequential compliance moment in a residential transaction. Rift makes it a visible lifecycle state rather than an offline side channel.

Every journey carries a representation status: **none**, **prepared**, **sent**, **signed**, **expired**, or **declined**, with effective and expiration dates where signed.

Rules:

- A buyer journey cannot advance past **Ready to shop**, and a seller journey cannot advance past **Pricing and launch**, while representation status is anything other than signed. Rift blocks the advance and explains why rather than silently allowing it.
- Rift prepares agreements and tracks their status. Sending for signature happens through the connected e-signature provider; Rift never signs, and never sends an agreement without the agent's explicit action.
- The client sees representation as a plain-language task with the agent as a visible participant, including what the agreement covers and its term.
- Expiration is a monitored deadline that raises attention before it lapses, not after.
- Agreement status, dates, and the signed document live in the relationship record and carry into the transaction file.

## Customer command center

The customer home screen prioritizes current lifecycle stage, one primary next action, and the nearest meaningful deadline. Supporting areas include the visual journey, tasks, documents, appointments, Decision Rooms, recent updates, household members, and help.

Every task names its owner: customer, agent, Rift, or outside professional. Milestones can be completed, current, upcoming, blocked, waiting, optional, or overdue.

## Agent command center

The daily brief and attention queue contain:

- Urgent and overdue work.
- Customers needing a personal response.
- New assessments and booked consultations.
- Offers awaiting a present-or-hold decision.
- Milestones reached.
- Decisions and changes awaiting approval.
- Missing documents and outside-professional blockers.
- Representation status approaching expiration.
- Upcoming appointments and deadlines.
- Failed workflows requiring manual action.
- Work Rift completed automatically.

Buyer pipeline, seller pipeline, calendar, client directory, playbook activity, source and channel reporting, and referral activity support deeper work. A universal command control can find clients, explain status, prepare work, and execute permitted actions using plain language.

## Client relationship model

One person or household can have multiple journeys and transactions. Buyer and seller journeys remain independent but can be connected when a purchase depends on a sale.

A relationship includes people and household roles, source and attribution, journeys, properties, transactions, representation status, tasks, milestones, deadlines, appointments, documents, permissions, communications, meeting recaps, decisions, playbooks, collaborators, vendors, referrals given and received, and activity history.

## Rift Offer

Rift Offer is open to anyone without an account or identity-verification step.

1. The submitter searches any address.
2. Rift-managed listings receive a confirmed listing-specific experience.
3. General addresses remain eligible and are labeled as unconfirmed by Rift.
4. The submitter uploads the signed offer PDF first.
5. Rift prepares the comparison fields from the document.
6. The submitter reviews and corrects the prepared information.
7. Rift asks whether proof of funds or preapproval is included and allows optional uploads.
8. Rift accepts incomplete submissions but flags missing, inconsistent, or uncertain information.
9. Original files, the prepared summary, timestamp, and activity history are preserved.
10. The offer enters the agent review queue.

Prepared information is grouped into Price, Financing, Deposits, Dates, Contingencies, Concessions, Documents, and Special Terms. A private confirmation link and reference number can support revision or withdrawal without an account.

### Agent-first review

Every offer reaches the agent before it reaches anyone else. There is no configuration that changes this.

On review, the agent takes one of three recorded decisions:

| Decision | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Present** | The offer is released into the seller's Decision Room with the agent's context attached |
| **Hold** | The offer remains with the agent, pending more information, a deadline, or a conversation; the submitter sees that it was received and is under review |
| **Decline to present** | The offer is not released; the agent records the reason and the seller instruction it rests on |

Rules:

- Every decision is timestamped and attributed, with the reason preserved. This record exists so the agent can demonstrate exactly what was received and what was done with it.
- Nothing is ever discarded. A declined offer remains in the file with its original documents.
- The submitter is always told their offer was received, with a reference number, whether or not it is presented.
- Held offers raise an attention item after four hours and escalate daily until resolved.
- Where a seller has given a standing instruction about which offers to present, that instruction is recorded on the listing and shown at review time.

### Submitters and unrepresented buyers

A submission is a relationship, not a form.

- Every submitter becomes a tracked contact with source recorded as Rift Offer.
- A cooperating agent becomes a professional contact, useful for future cooperation and for the agent's network.
- A submitter who indicates they are unrepresented is flagged immediately and routed to the agent, never to an automated sequence. The agent decides how to proceed, and Rift presents the options plainly: refer them to another agent, discuss representation, or proceed as an unrepresented party with the disclosures that requires.
- Rift never provides advice to an unrepresented submitter, never characterizes the strength of their offer to them, and never implies representation. The interface states plainly who Rift's agent represents.
- Unrepresented submissions on unconfirmed addresses receive a clear message that Rift does not represent the seller of that property and that the submission has been recorded but may not reach anyone.

### Abuse and integrity

An account-free public intake needs defenses that do not punish the honest submitter.

- Rate limiting per address, per network origin, and per contact detail, with a clear human path when a legitimate submitter is caught.
- File type, size, and content validation before any preparation runs.
- Duplicate detection across submissions on the same property, surfaced as related rather than blocked.
- Submissions on unconfirmed addresses are held in a separate, clearly labeled queue with no notification to any property owner.
- No property owner, occupant, or address is ever contacted as a result of an offer submission on an unconfirmed address.
- Preparation cost per submission is capped; beyond the cap, the submitter completes fields manually and the offer is still accepted.

## Document understanding

Document preparation is not a background convenience. It sits on the critical path of Rift Offer in the MVP, and it is the capability most likely to produce a wrong number in front of a client. It gets first-class treatment.

Every prepared field carries:

| Attribute | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Value | What Rift read |
| Confidence | High, medium, or low, shown to whoever must confirm it |
| Source | The document and page the value came from, linked where practical |
| State | Prepared, corrected by submitter, confirmed by agent, or unresolved |

Rules:

- The original file is never modified and is always retrievable.
- Low-confidence and contradictory values are surfaced for explicit resolution, not silently accepted.
- Consequential fields — price, financing type, deposit amounts, binding dates, contingency deadlines — always require human confirmation regardless of confidence.
- Any preparation failure degrades to manual entry with the fields visible and empty. The submission still completes.
- Contradictions between documents, and between a document and existing journey data, become explicit flags with both values shown.
- Preparation is never the last word on a date that drives a deadline; the agent confirms it before it becomes a monitored obligation.

## Decision Rooms

A Decision Room contains the decision and deadline, available scenarios, comparable numbers, assumptions, benefits, trade-offs, risks, source documents, review state, agent context, customer questions, and recorded decision.

Initial Decision Rooms focus on buyer affordability and seller offer comparison. Mature Decision Rooms include property comparison, offer strategy, inspection response, concessions, closing options, and connected sale-and-purchase decisions.

## Meeting-to-Plan

1. Capture typed notes, uploaded notes, or a consented recording or transcript.
2. Identify facts, decisions, commitments, concerns, missing information, and follow-ups.
3. Compare findings with the current profile and plan.
4. Propose field changes, milestones, tasks, dates, documents, and communications.
5. Show a before-and-after agent review.
6. Publish approved changes to the customer command center.
7. Execute permitted follow-up and monitor completion.

Meeting recaps distinguish observed statements, proposed changes, and approved plan updates.

Meeting-to-Plan is the mechanism that makes consultation booking worth doing, so it belongs in the earliest usable product rather than a later phase.

## Document-to-Journey

Document processing preserves the original file and presents extracted information as a reviewable preparation layer, following the document understanding rules above. Prepared fields should link to their source page where practical. Contradictions, missing attachments, uncertain extraction, and consequential dates require review.

The earliest usable product demonstrates a single high-value path end to end: a binding contract becomes proposed dates, responsibilities, and tasks that the agent approves into the client journey. Classification, addendum checks, duplicate detection, and email ingestion expand later.

## Lifecycle Playbooks

Canonical stages remain fixed; playbooks customize execution inside them.

A playbook defines scope, trigger, conditions, timing, actions, authority, recovery, and history. Actions can include tasks, drafts, messages, appointments, document requests, vendor coordination, gifts, surveys, referrals, and internal notifications.

Playbooks can run manually, with approval, or automatically. Purchases require approved vendors, confirmed shipping information, per-order and monthly limits, receipts, and configurable approval thresholds. Failures retry safely, alert the agent, and create a manual fallback task.

## Referral and reputation engine

A closing that produces no review, no referral path, and no reason to return is an incomplete closing. Rift makes advocacy a designed workflow with the same rigor as a deadline.

**The moments.** Advocacy is asked for when the client is genuinely happy, not on a fixed schedule. Rift recognizes four:

1. A meaningful obstacle resolved mid-journey, such as an accepted offer or a cleared financing condition.
2. Closing day.
3. Two weeks after move-in, once the disruption has passed.
4. The one-year anniversary of ownership.

**The review path.** A prepared, personal request the agent approves before it sends, linking directly to the platform where the review matters. Non-response gets exactly one follow-up, then stops. A client who signals dissatisfaction is never asked for a public review; that becomes an agent attention item instead.

**The referral path.** Every past client has a personal referral link and a simple way to introduce someone. A referred person arrives at a page that names the person who sent them. The referral is credited to the referrer automatically on the new relationship record.

**Acknowledgement.** Every referral received produces a prompt to thank the referrer, and every referral that becomes a closing produces a second one. Rift never lets a referral go unacknowledged.

**Measurement.** Reviews requested and received, referrals given and received, referral-to-close rate, and advocacy share of new leads are reported per client and in aggregate.

**Boundaries.** No incentive that would be an unlawful inducement or an undisclosed payment for a referral. No review gating, no pre-screening for positive sentiment before allowing a review, and no automated sending of any advocacy request without agent approval.

## Rift Property Passport

After closing, the transaction becomes a durable property record: documents, systems, appliances, warranties, manuals, receipts, inspections, improvements, vendors, and maintenance history. It gives the client a reason to return without being asked to, and gives the agent a legitimate reason to stay present across the years between transactions.

## Agentic operating loop

1. **Observe** a new assessment, message, meeting, document, date, or event.
2. **Understand** the relevant client and transaction context.
3. **Plan** the next action and expected result.
4. **Check authority** against permissions, safeguards, and spending limits.
5. **Prepare or execute** the action.
6. **Verify** success or identify failure.
7. **Record** the complete activity.
8. **Improve** a reusable skill only through agent-approved learning.

Foundational infrastructure includes an agent runtime, MCP-compatible tool connections, durable workflows, versioned skills, scoped memory, permissions, audit history, monitoring, evaluation, cost controls, and manual fallback.

## Autonomy settings

The three operating modes from the vision are a real, visible surface, not a philosophy. Settings expose:

- A global mode: review everything, balanced, or high automation.
- Per-workflow authority: manual, prepare and wait for approval, or automatic.
- Per-client overrides, so a sensitive relationship can be handled more conservatively than the default.
- Per-channel authority, separating internal actions from anything a client will see.
- Spending authority: per-order limit, monthly limit, approved vendor list, and an approval threshold.
- A protected list of actions that remain manual in every mode: presenting or declining to present an offer, sending or signing an agreement, stating a price opinion, and any communication asserting a legal or lending conclusion.
- A visible record of what Rift did under each authority level, so the agent can raise or lower trust based on evidence.

## Communication

The earliest usable product may connect email while calls and text messages remain manual. Agents can quickly record a call or text touchpoint. Rift may prepare drafts, but the agent chooses how to send them.

The mature product can unify connected email, SMS, calls, notes, appointments, and workflow activity in one chronological relationship timeline.

All outbound client communication respects consent, records the basis for contact, and honors opt-out immediately across every channel and playbook.

## Access and trust

- Customers can access and export their personal journey information.
- Agents retain transaction and business records according to applicable requirements.
- Access follows least privilege across household members and collaborators.
- Sensitive documents require encryption, restricted access, and access history.
- Connected-service permissions are visible and revocable.
- Meeting recording requires clear participant consent.
- Protected characteristics and their proxies do not influence ranking or prioritization, under the enforcement rules in [vision.md](vision.md).
- Important failures create a visible alert and manual fallback task.
- Estimates show assumptions, source, date, and review state.

## Product economics

Agents and brokerages are the paying users. Core buyer and seller journey access is free. Referral or partner compensation must be disclosed and must not determine customer guidance.

Because clients are free and several capabilities carry per-use cost, Rift enforces cost discipline as a product rule.

The structural protection comes first: **front-end value is computed, not generated.** Instant tools, packages, assistance matching, and every figure a stranger sees are deterministic calculations against a verified registry. Their marginal cost is effectively zero, which is what makes it possible to overload the front end and leave it open to the public at the same time. Generative cost exists only where a human is already in the loop.

Working ceilings, to be revised against real usage:

| Capability | Trigger | Per-unit budget | Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instant tools and packages | Public, unauthenticated | Near zero, computed | None needed |
| Offer document preparation | Public, unauthenticated | Under $0.50 per submission | 3 per address per day; 40 per month across unconfirmed addresses |
| Contract and document preparation | Agent-initiated | Under $0.50 per document | 20 per relationship per month |
| Meeting processing | Agent-initiated | Under $1.00 per meeting | 8 per relationship per month |
| Agentic execution | Playbook-driven | Under $0.25 per action | $5 per relationship per month |
| Everything combined | — | — | $8 per active relationship per month |

Rules:

- Usage and cost are visible to the agent, per client and in total, with the current month tracked against the ceiling rather than reported after the fact.
- Reaching a ceiling degrades to manual entry with a clear message. It never blocks a submission, loses a document, or drops a deadline.
- Public, account-free surfaces carry the tightest ceilings, since they are the ones a stranger can trigger. Rift Offer's public intake is the single most abusable surface in the product and is budgeted as such.
- A ceiling reached is an agent attention item, not a silent failure, because a legitimate busy month and an abuse attempt look identical until someone looks.
- These figures are hypotheses. The point is that the ceilings exist and are watched from the first day, not that these are the right numbers.
